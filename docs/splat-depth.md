# Camera depth

Open **Display → Camera Depth** to inspect the current viewport camera. Near
surfaces are lighter and distant surfaces are darker. The display responds to
the orbit target distance and keeps the background darker than visible
geometry. Toggle **Camera Depth** again to return to the regular viewport.
The display mode is session state. Switching it creates no document edit.

Pause playback and finish active editing gestures before choosing
**Display → Export Camera Depth**. Export works from either the color or depth
view. Use **Cancel Depth Export** or Escape during capture to stop the download.
Changing the scene, frame or named camera view cancels an export in progress.
An orbit or dolly before capture uses the current camera at the capture frame.
After capture begins, the export keeps that camera's sampled matrices.
No project settings or selection are saved or modified by export.

## Depth convention

- Depth is positive camera-axis distance, equal to minus the camera-local Z
  coordinate, in unitless scene units. Off-axis pixels use the same axis.
- A splat contributes where its individual effective Gaussian fragment alpha
  reaches 0.5 after Spark's footprint and falloff. The nearest qualifying
  projected Gaussian center contributes depth. Overlapping splats below the
  threshold remain background even if their combined color looks opaque.
- The pass uses the current displayed Spark evaluation, including authored
  object/component transforms, pose, visibility, opacity and resident LoD.
  It waits for the displayed accumulation and sort to settle before export.
- Visible depth-writing opaque and alpha-cutout mesh slots participate.
  Opacity-blended slots, grids, wire overlays, rig helpers, curves and particles
  are excluded.
- Zero represents background, clipped geometry and depths rounded to the far
  clear value. The camera near/far values are in the metadata.
- The temporary depth attachment uses standard forward 32-bit floating-point
  hardware depth. Orthographic quantization is close to uniform. Perspective
  precision falls with distance. At near 0.01, depth-buffer quantization alone
  can contribute about 0.0003 units at distance 10 and 0.03 units at 100.
  Export uses a linear float raster and retains the input depth precision.
- The raster uses the viewport drawing-buffer aspect and resolution, limited
  proportionally to 2048 pixels on the larger axis. A smaller GPU limit also
  applies. Preview and export use the same depth sampling.
  The editor keeps one capture in flight and at most one completed download URL.
  Starting another download releases the previous URL. A completed URL expires
  after one minute.

## Output

The ZIP contains a single-channel Portable Float Map named `depth.pfm` and
`metadata.json`. PFM starts with `Pf`, the width and height, and a scale of
`-1.0` indicating little-endian float32 values. Pixels run left-to-right from
the bottom row to the top. Metadata includes the frame, scene time, camera
view, clip distances, resolution, projection and camera matrices, and the
alpha/background convention.

Use the half-integer pixel center to compute NDC X/Y, with row zero at the
bottom. Unproject that X/Y at NDC Z=-1 through `projectionMatrixInverse`. In
perspective, multiply the camera-space point by exported depth divided by its
negative Z. In orthographic projection, replace that point's Z with negative
exported depth. Apply `cameraMatrixWorld` to obtain scene coordinates.
Matrices are column-major. Skip pixels with a depth of zero.

## Reference scene

Open `examples/splat-depth-study.msb`, generated by
`node scripts/create-splat-depth-example.mjs`. Its seven original splats and
one opaque plane cover 6–24 scene units. Frame 0 has the skinned splat at 7.75
units and frame 24 places it at 6.5 units in the saved camera. A component and
object transform combine to place another splat at 7.2 units. The opaque plane
writes depth at 6 units in front of a splat at 8. The two translucent splats
overlap in color and yield background depth. The fixture uses no external
assets.

The Display menu follows Maya's viewport render-channel workflow and uses an
ordinary checkable menu item. Musubi provides a transient viewport diagnostic
and a dedicated depth raster. [Maya Arnold Viewport Rendering](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/AR-Maya/files/am-Arnold_for_Maya_User_Guide/rendering/viewport_render_html.html) [Maya Z Depth AOV](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/AR-Maya/files/am-Arnold_for_Maya_User_Guide/tutorials/am-AOV_Tutorials/arnold_for_maya_aovs_am_Z_Depth_AOV_html.html)

The pass reuses Spark 2.4.0 (MIT), Three 0.180.0 (MIT) and the existing fflate
0.8.3 dependency (MIT). Its depth pass, raster packaging and fixture are
original. Spark's cube-map example demonstrates RGB encoding of nonlinear
projection depth using a splat modifier. [Spark cube-depth example](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark/blob/a7df322e1e1b18ec296192509862e01b21189bc8/examples/render-cube-depth/index.html) [SparkRenderer documentation](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/spark-renderer/)
