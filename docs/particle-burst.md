# Particle Burst Baker

Open **Particle Burst Baker** from the Musubi Tools launcher, or visit `/tools/particle-burst/` on the editor's server. **Load Example** opens the original Flash, Sparks, and Afterburst recipe.

1. In **Stages**, add, duplicate, rename, move, or remove emitters. Each stage has a start time in seconds and its own seed and burst settings. **Enabled for Preview and Bake** controls the authored effect. **Solo Preview** lets you inspect the selected stage, including a disabled stage. Baking always combines the enabled stages in list order.
2. Set the selected emitter in **Emission**, motion in **Dynamics**, and start and end appearance in **Shading**. A cone half-angle of 180° emits in every direction. Azimuth turns from +X toward +Z. Elevation measures up from the XZ plane. Positive gravity accelerates downward.
3. Play at 24 fps or enter a frame number, including fractional frames. Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, or use **Frame Burst** to fit the visible particles. Middle-drag a numeric value for live preview and release to commit one edit. Escape restores the value and frame at the start of the edit. Undo and Redo cover recipe changes, including opening a file.
4. Click **Bake Frame to PLY** and import the file into the Musubi editor as a splat object. **Save Recipe** writes a version 2 compound JSON file. **Open Recipe** also accepts a version 1 recipe as one enabled stage starting at zero.

The evaluator samples each particle directly at the requested time from its fixed birth time and seeded launch velocity. Births are evenly spaced across the emission duration. With duration zero they start simultaneously. Each particle has the same lifetime. Size, peak opacity, and the numerical sRGB channels interpolate linearly over age. The PLY stores world-space positions. The editor applies its usual centering transform on import. Each exported Gaussian is isotropic with standard deviation equal to half the authored diameter. PLY writes 3DGS DC color coefficients, logit opacity, natural-log scales and WXYZ rotation.

The preview shows a transparent Gaussian point approximation out to three standard deviations. Large points can be limited by the graphics hardware, and compositing can differ from the editor's splat renderer. The baked file stores a single static sample. Export skips invisible particles and is unavailable on an empty frame. Recipes are separate from MSB projects.

Each recipe supports 1–8 stages and up to 12,000 total births, including disabled stages. A stage can start up to 30 seconds into the effect, emit over up to 10 seconds, and give each particle up to 20 seconds of lifetime. The maximum stage timeline is 60 seconds. A particle is included at its birth and excluded at its death. Reordering stages changes PLY row order. IDs and stage seeds keep individual samples stable across reorder and unrelated edits. Compound files are capped at 16 KiB. Version 1 files retain the 4 KiB cap.

At the maximum count, the active sampler holds up to 672,000 bytes for velocities and one reusable sample buffer. The preview holds up to 384,000 bytes each for CPU and graphics attributes. These bounds cover particle arrays and exclude the canvas, browser overhead, undo recipes, and the temporary PLY payload.

The stage list and selected-stage controls follow Maya's Outliner and Attribute Editor organization. Start time and independent seeds map to Maya's particle Time Attributes and Emission Random Stream Seeds. The baker samples directly at the chosen frame, so edits appear immediately when you scrub backward or forward.
