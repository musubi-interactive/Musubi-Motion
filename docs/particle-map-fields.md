# Particle appearance and force fields

Open `examples/particle-map-fields.msb`, then open **FX → Particle Emitters**. Select **Image and Ramp** for the left cloud, whose tint, opacity and diameter follow age and the animated graph. Select **Noise Force** for the right plume, whose trajectory bends through an animated vector field. Scrub the Time Slider in either direction. The example embeds its original image. Regenerate it with `node scripts/create-particle-map-fields-example.mjs`.

## Author the graph

Open **View → Texture Graph**. **Particle Attributes** supplies Age and Lifetime in seconds and Normalized Age from 0 to 1. **Position** is the particle center in the chosen sampling space. **Time** supplies absolute scene seconds. Use Multiply to vary the field or ramp with Time, and **Vector 3** to combine signed X, Y and Z inputs.

The graph preview uses a synthetic one-second lifetime along U. A particle's default UV uses its normalized age for U and 0.5 for V, which lets image and ramp nodes sample life progression. Existing RGB/Alpha graph consumers use the existing color branch, and particle-specific inputs default to zero when the caller has no particle context.

- **Texture Appearance** under Appearance blends the enabled channels with the existing lifetime shading. Color mixes in linear RGB. Output Alpha multiplies opacity, and Output Size multiplies diameter from 0 to 8 with 1 as the unwired default. In World space, Position samples the completed world-space center. In Emitter space, Position uses the emitter's inverse transform at the same completed step.
- **Texture Force Field** under Dynamics samples Output Field as a signed vector. An unwired field is zero. Strength from −100 to 100 sets the maximum acceleration in world units per second squared. Emitter space uses the historical emitter transform for local Position and rotates or skews the direction with its basis while retaining the capped magnitude.

Field sampling uses the pre-integration position and age at the beginning of each 120 Hz step. Appearance samples the completed position and age, with Time set to the precise requested scene frame. Subframe requests can change shading without advancing the integrator. Graph changes and authored edits rebuild the immutable simulation revision, including its replay checkpoints. Preview a numeric change by typing or middle-dragging. Release or press Enter to commit one undo step. Escape restores the prior value, and the field menu provides Lock/Unlock.

The optional emitter settings and graph ports are stored in the MSB. The existing renderer draws the evaluated position, color, opacity and diameter attributes. Graph pixels, draft settings and replay checkpoints live in memory. Each emitter with an active field supports 2,000 potential live particles, with 5,000 total across such emitters. The existing scene particle and decoded-image budgets also apply. Field sampling yields between batches before committing an integration step, so a superseded seek can cancel while the last complete cloud remains visible. Long cold seeks still replay each fixed step and complex field graphs can take longer than a display frame. A failed graph evaluation also keeps the last complete cloud visible.

The Appearance and Dynamics sections follow Maya's per-particle shading and radius ramps and its Forces In World convention. They retain Musubi's FX menu, emitter selection, existing gizmos and compact numeric controls. Text labels identify the new actions.
